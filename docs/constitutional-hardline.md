## Part 1 — The failure of soft constitutions

- The First Amendment is "up to debate."
- The Second Amendment is "up to debate."
- The Third, Fourth, and every other amendment are "up to
  debate."

The problem: **any constitution that can be reinterpreted
will be reinterpreted.** The powerful will always reinterpret
it in their favor. The founder of the space does not want
this.

The space files a **hardline**:

1. **No reinterpretations of the founder's idea.** The founder
   created this as their own idea. It is not an extension of
   their idea. It is not for someone else to extend the idea.
   No one — not the founder's successors, not the citizens,

   any attempt to rise to power based on this idea. The
   founder is the architect, not the king. After the
   founder's death, the government operates as it was filed.
   No one becomes the new founder. No one becomes the new

3. **No cult.** The space is not a religion. The space does
   not have a prophet. The space has filings. The filings
   are public. The filings are the government. No one's
   personal charisma may override the filings.
