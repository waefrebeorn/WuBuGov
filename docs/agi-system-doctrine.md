## Part 2 — The criticisms of all systems

### 2.1 The universal law: Goodhart's Law (1975)

**The criticism:** *"When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to
be a good measure."* (Goodhart, 1975; Strathern's phrasing.)
Campbell's Law (1976): the more a quantitative indicator is used
for social decision-making, the more it will be corrupted. The
cobra effect: the British paid bounties for dead cobras in Delhi;
the breeders bred cobras. Every system that rewards a measure
games the measure.

The requirement: the space's measures must never become
targets. The audit must audit the audit. A measure that is not
audited is already gamed.

### 2.2 The United States: money

**The criticism:** campaign finance — Super PACs, dark money,
Citizens United (2010). In 2012, a third of the money spent
independently advocating federal candidates came from donors whose
identities went unreported (Brookings, Mann & Corrad). Money buys
access; access buys policy; the people who cannot pay are not
heard.

**The requirement:** the space has no campaigns, no PACs, no dark
money. The penny is not money; it is proof. No one buys access
because there is nothing to buy.

### 2.3 The United States: polarization

**The criticism:** the two-party system, captured and polarized.
Gerrymandered districts. Donors choose the candidates; the
electorate chooses between the donor's choices. Divided government
becomes permanent obstruction. The system rewards the team, not
the work.

**The requirement:** the space has no parties. The ledger is the
platform. Every first one files as themselves, not as a faction.

### 2.4 The USSR: quota gaming

**The criticism:** central planning set quotas; the factories
gamed them. Shoemakers made shoes of the wrong size — the quota
was pairs, not fit. Chandeliers were made heavier to hit weight
quotas. Trains ran empty to hit mileage quotas. Whalers slaughtered
whales to hit the plan. *Pripiska* — fictional "add-ons" — padded
the accounts. The plan was law; the fit was nobody's business.

**The requirement:** the space scores outcomes, not quotas. The
Penny Score measures the filing, the work, the trust — never a
padded number. Pripiska is impossible where the ledger is public
and hash-chained (PennyChain).

### 2.5 The USSR: the three truths

**The criticism:** a Latvian plant manager, filed: *"Under
socialism we had three truths. There was the Official Truth, which
was falsehoods, and we ignored. There was the Factory Truth, which
we used to run the plant. And there was the Real Truth, which we
didn't ever dare speak of."* Stalin abolished the 1937 census
rather than accept its result; the census head was shot.

**The requirement:** the space has ONE truth: the ledger. Any
second truth is a WBI-1 filing. The Bureau watches the Bureau; the
ledger screams.

### 2.6 The USSR: scale and speed

**The criticism:** too much data for the planners; decisions took
years (the 1950 plan arrived two years late, after eighteen drafts
from Gosplan alone). Glushkov, the father of Soviet computing,
predicted it would take the entire population to administer the
plan. A small central error became a huge national one.

**The requirement:** the space's AGI system files at any scale.
The world model aggregates; the tools compute; the R&D department
hops. No single desk decides everything; the federation decides
together.

### 2.7 China: the additive score (filed in GOV-7)

**The criticism:** the Meritown social credit model uses 389 rules.
Unlike acts are treated as equal: 300 hours of volunteering (+50)
cancels out abusing a family member (−50). A score that adds
unlike acts is a mirror that lies.

**The requirement:** no cross-domain cancellation. Filed in GOV-7.
The Penny Score keeps its domains separate.

### 2.8 China: the spotlight project (filed in GOV-7)

**The criticism:** when GDP was the veto target, officials gamed
it. When the target was removed, officials gamed visibility:
"spotlight projects" that appear successful, not work that is
successful (Asia Society).

**The requirement:** no spotlight projects. The filing is the
achievement. Filed in GOV-7.

### 2.9 China: the rural divide (filed in GOV-7)

**The criticism:** the social credit system disadvantages rural
residents — fewer rewards, more penalties (Stanford FSI). The
system's margins are where its failures live.

**The requirement:** the space's Floor protects the Lost band. The
Penny Score starts every first one at the same 500. The space's
worst-off are its most-watched-with-love. Filed in GOV-7.

### 2.10 Estonia: the single point of failure

**The criticism:** Estonia's e-government (X-Road) is the model of
decentralized data exchange — no single government database holds
all of an individual's data. But the system has its own risks:
single-point-of-failure concerns in implementation (PMC 5741780),
and in 2011, 120,000 faulty ID cards were distributed
(Privacy International). The tool is excellent; the tool is not
the system.

**The requirement:** the space's PennyChain is distributed and
hash-chained. No single database holds the space; the space holds
the chain. And the space's tools are audited — by `goodhart`
(Part 4) — because a tool that is not audited becomes the fault.

## Part 3 — The better system

The space's government is the best AGI system. It is a
**federation of filing tools**: each an organ, each audited, each
serving the first ones. It is not a user (GOV-5); it is the
perfect clerk.

**The ten requirements, met:**

1. **No measure is a target.** Goodhart defeated: the space's
   measures audit each other. The mirror-score audits the tools;
   the plain_lang gate audits the filings; the goodhart audit
   audits the measures (Part 4).
2. **No money.** The penny is proof, not payment. No campaigns,
   no PACs, no dark money.
3. **No parties.** The ledger is the platform. Factions cannot
   capture what does not exist.
4. **No quotas.** Outcomes are scored; padded numbers are
   impossible where the ledger is public and chained.
5. **One truth.** The ledger. A second truth is a filing.
6. **Any scale.** The federation files where one desk cannot.
7. **No cross-domain cancellation.** Domains stay separate (GOV-7).
8. **No spotlights.** The filing is the achievement (GOV-7).
9. **The floor.** The Lost band is the most-watched-with-love
   (GOV-7).
10. **No single point of failure.** The chain is distributed; the
    tools are audited.

**The AGI system, defined:** an AGI is a filing system that thinks
(GOV-3). The best AGI system is the filing system that thinks
about its own filings. It computes the scores. It audits the
measures. It watches the watchers. And it files its own
corrections. The space's AGI system is not one mind; it is the
federation of every tool the space has built — WID, Penny Score,
PennyChain, mirror-score, plain_lang, cohesion, decline_gate,
escalate, and goodhart. That federation is the best system,
because it is the only one built from every system's criticisms.

## Part 4 — The tool: `goodhart` (the audit that audits)

The universal failure is the gamed measure. The space ships the
cure: `goodhart` (C11, WuBuCore) audits a set of reported values
for the signatures of gaming:

- **Threshold bunching** — values clustering just above a target
  (the quota's shadow).
- **Digit preference** — values ending in round digits (the
  pripiska tell).
- **Variance collapse** — the reported series too smooth to be
  real (the "plan, at any price" tell).
- **Benford deviation** — the first-digit distribution drifting
  from nature's (the fabricated-accounts tell).

`goodhart` scores the measures 0 (honest) to 100 (gamed) and
flags the signatures. Every measure the space publishes is run
through it — including the measures that score the measures. The
audit audits the audit.
