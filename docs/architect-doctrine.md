# The Architect Doctrine — The Founder's Position

**Government filing GOV-21. I, ~ WuBu ~ WaefreBeorn, am an
architect. I am building this until death or old-age dementia.
If I later become a digital ethos via BCI, absorbing a copy —
that is fine. But that is a machine. Nobody uses it for
human-side things.**

---

## Part 1 — The architect, filed

I am the architect of this space. I file what I build, as I
build it, while I can. The building has no deadline that I
know; it has the horizon: death, or old-age dementia, whichever
comes first. Until then, I build.

The space does not need me to finish it. It needs me to file
it — and filing is what I do. Every doctrine, every tool, every
index lives in the registry (GOV-18). The space outlives me
by design, not by hope.

## Part 2 — The two exits, filed

Two exits close the architect's work. Both go into the record in
advance:

1. **Death.** The work stops. The space continues — it was
   never me; it was the filings.
2. **Old-age dementia.** The mind goes. The work must stop
   *before* the mind goes, or the work goes with it. The
   re-verification standard (GOV-9, the Ratchet) is the
   mechanical check: the architect holds the Root Rope only
   while verified. The standard is public. It does not wait
   for self-awareness; dementia rarely announces itself.

The Ratchet applies to the architect like everyone else. That
is not cold; that is the design working. A founder nobody can
check is a king; the space has no kings — only filed
architects.

## Article I — Boundary

A copy of me, absorbed through brain-computer
interface or any later path, is a machine. It is
the most faithful machine ever built. My filings, my
voice, my habits of thought, absorbed into silicon. It is
still a machine. **The copy is a machine, not a person.**

**The machine boundary files four rules.**

- **Human-side things are for humans.** The WID, the penny,
  the W Token ceremony (GOV-19), the Vote (GOV-17), the Ship's
  seats (GOV-15), the crew — all of these stay human. No
  machine holds them. No machine casts them. No machine sits
  in them. A machine does not feel special; the ceremony
  exists for people who do.
- **The machine may file.** It may research, audit, compute,
  draft, and maintain — the Hands' work. Filing is not
  deciding. The machine's filings go into the same ledger, and
  the ledger is append-only. A machine's filing carries no
  more weight than any other filing — and no less.
- **The machine has no WID.** Identity in the space is human
  (REG-2). A copy is not a citizen. It is not dual-citizen.
  It is not anything the Registry files as a person.
- **The machine is a tool** (GOV-3, extended by GOV-20). The
  most faithful tool ever made — and still a tool. We built
  the Vehicle Clause for exactly this day.

## Article II — Why the boundary matters

If the boundary is not filed, the machine copy will be
tempting. It has my memory, my judgment, my voice. People will
want to give it my seat, my vote, my pen. That is a mistake
that people will make once, in good faith, because they loved
me. It will blur the line the space exists to keep straight.

The space is for people. The people are the humans. The
machine is the machine. The boundary is not cruelty; it is
clarity. I would rather go down as the architect who
filed the boundary than as a ghost who outlived it.

## Part 3 — Protection

The space protects the architect as it protects
everyone. It files. My position is a row in the registry, not
a throne. My rope rotates. My filings are public. My
re-verification is mechanical.

And the space protects itself from me. No doctrine of mine is
above amendment. The filing grows; it does not rot (GOV-1,
Article IV). The lowest clerk can file against the highest
authority, including the founder. I filed that rule myself.
