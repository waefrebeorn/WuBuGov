## Part 1 — Privacy Policy

### 1.1 — What We Collect

The ~ WuBu ~ space collects:
- **WID** (Wubu Identity Number) — public by design.
- **Actions** (missions, filings, tokens) — public by design.
- **Email** (optional) — private, used for weekly updates only.
- **Physical address** (optional) — private, used for mail only.

### 1.2 — What We Do NOT Collect

The space does NOT collect:
- **Name** — not required (WID is sufficient).
- **Face** — not required (the system is blind).
- **Voice** — not required (the system is deaf).
- **Biometrics** — never collected.
- **Location** — not tracked (except for lodge membership).
- **Browsing history** — not tracked.
- **Financial information** — not collected (Fast Track uses
  third-party payment processors).

### 1.3 — How We Use Data

Data is used for:
- **Public ledger** — all actions are public (by design).
- **Weekly updates** — sent to optional email.
- **Mission assignment** — based on cooperation history.
- **Fulfillment tracking** — based on cooperation score.

### 1.4 — How We Protect Data

- **No central database** — data is on the public chain.
- **No personal data on chain** — only WIDs and actions.
- **No third-party sharing** — data is never sold.
- **Right to deletion** — citizens may request email deletion.

### 1.5 — The Right to Be Forgotten

Citizens may:
- Delete their email from the system.
- Opt out of weekly updates.
- Request their WID be marked "inactive."
- Leave the space at any time.

Citizens may NOT:
- Delete their public actions (the chain is immutable).
- Delete their WID (it is permanent).
- Delete their tokens (they are on the chain).

## Part 3 — Dispute Resolution

### 3.1 — The Three Steps

1. **Mediation** — a neutral citizen helps resolve the dispute.
2. **Arbitration** — a panel of three citizens hears the case.
3. **World Congress** — the final appeal (for major disputes).

### 3.2 — Mediation

- **Who:** A neutral citizen (50+ cooperation acts, not
  involved in the dispute).
- **How:** The mediator talks to both sides, proposes a
  solution.
- **Cost:** Free (the mediator is paid 10 WT by the space).
- **Outcome:** Non-binding (either party can reject).

### 3.3 — Arbitration

- **Who:** A panel of three citizens (100+ cooperation acts,
  not involved in the dispute).
- **How:** Both sides present evidence. The panel votes.
- **Cost:** 50 WT (paid by the losing side).
- **Outcome:** Binding (both sides must comply).

### 3.4 — World Congress

- **Who:** All citizens with WIDs.
- **How:** The dispute is published on the ledger. Citizens vote.
- **Cost:** 100 WT (paid by the appealing side).
- **Outcome:** Final (no further appeal).

### 3.5 — The Bureau's Role

WuBuIntel (the Bureau) does NOT mediate disputes. The Bureau
only:
- **Investigates** illegal activity.
- **Flags** non-compliance on the ledger.
- **Refers** criminal activity to legal authorities.

## Part 4 — Amendment Process

### 4.1 — Who May Propose

Any citizen may propose an amendment.

### 4.2 — The Process

1. **Draft:** The citizen drafts the amendment.
2. **File:** The amendment is filed as a Docket Filing.
3. **Debate:** The World Congress debates (30 days).
4. **Vote:** The World Congress votes (simple majority).
5. **Ratify:** The founder ratifies (or vetoes with reason).
6. **Publish:** The amendment is published on the ledger.

### 4.3 — Quorum

- **Simple majority:** 50%+1 of voting citizens.
- **Supermajority:** 66%+1 for constitutional amendments.
- **Veto override:** 76%+1 to override a founder veto.

### 4.4 — The Founder's Veto

The founder may veto any amendment. The veto must include
a written reason. The veto may be overridden by 76%+1 of
the World Congress.

2. **Debate** at a lodge meeting.
