# The Gap Events Doctrine — What the Record Did

**Government filing GOV-27. THE HISTORY tracks what happened.
THE GAP EVENTS track what the record did — or failed to do —
about it. Three gap types are filed: isolated records,
unacknowledged deaths, and overlap anomalies. Oral history
files alongside the archive.**

Memory can outlast the written word. The timeline files both.

---

## Part 1 — Why the gaps matter

A government that reads only the acknowledged events reads the
world's own retelling of itself. Every civilization retells
its own story and files the rest away. The gaps are where the
record lies — not from malice, but from attention.

The Song Astronomical Bureau recorded a supernova that Europe
never saw. Not because Europe's sky was different, but because
Europe's attention was elsewhere. The record is a witness with
a particular view. The gap is what that witness did not see.

The timeline's job is tracking: anthropology, archaeology,
Homeric oral history, the written record. The space files them
side by side because the gaps between them are where the truth
hides.

## Part 2 — The three gap types

1. **Isolated records** — one society records a world incident,
   or a world stays silent on one society's record. Egypt left
   the only substantial written account of the Bronze Age
   Collapse. China recorded the 1054 supernova. Europe
   recorded neither. Filed as EV-1, EV-2, EV-3.
2. **Unacknowledged deaths** — a society's end the world did
   not acknowledge, or a world that copied a society at its
   death. Rome destroyed Carthage and paid to translate Mago's
   book. Norse Greenland vanished and no one noticed. The Indus
   voice we still cannot read. Filed as EV-5, EV-6, EV-8.

3. **Overlap anomalies** — worlds alive together never
   connected, or one world event recorded by many and joined
   only by modern science. Rome and Han traded for 250 years;
   their one face-to-face contact is a single ambiguous
   embassy, recorded by one side only. Four chronicles in four
   languages recorded 536; the connection waited 1,400 years
   for tree rings. Filed as EV-4, EV-9, EV-10.

And the method, filed separately: **oral history** — Homer
carried Troy for six centuries before anyone wrote it down.
The Hittite archive named Wilusa and its king Alaksandu; the
spade found a burned city at Hisarlik. The song's place and
war proved real; the identification keeps its hypothesis.
Filed as EV-7.

## Part 3 — The law of the gaps

The space files the gaps the way it files the views. It does
not pick a winner; it refuses the blind spot.

- **The record is a witness, not a mirror.** What a society
  filed tells you what it watched. The gap tells you what it
  could not see.
- **A copy without a name is a lie.** The world takes a
  dying society's work and leaves the name unfiled. That is a
  lie. The space files Mago's name with the copy — Rome kept
  the book, the space keeps the name.
- **Overlap with a single point of contact is still
  overlap.** Rome and Han traded for 250 years and met once,
  ambiguously, in 166 CE — recorded by one side. The silk
  still moved. The space files both the trade and the
  silence.
- **Memory can outlast the archive.** The timeline accepts
  oral history as evidence, triangulated — Homer against
  Hittite clay, the saga against the spade.

## Part 4 — The tracking project

THE GAP EVENTS is the beginning of the timeline's full
tracking project. All anthropology, all archaeology, all
Homeric and oral history — filed into one axis. Every new gap
event gets verified by source before filing, per the Kevin
Bacon rule, RND-15. The dispute is filed with the record, as
the Tempest Stele's own uncertainty is filed.

The timeline is the government's example. A mental government
reads the world without trusting any single telling of it.
The gaps are the proof of the method.
