# The Human-Centered Doctrine — Tools, Not Companions

**Constitutional filing GOV-3. The space's official position on
artificial intelligence, AGI, and the human mirror problem.
Filed after triple-DA research (AUD-2, RND-18).**

**AMENDED BY: GOV-5 (User Doctrine) Article I; GOV-6 (Integration
Doctrine) Part 3 — see those filings for the amendments.**

---

## Article I — What AI is (the tool doctrine)

> *"Automated general intelligence and AGI and AI is a tool built
> by humanity to help expand."*

**Filed: AI is a tool.** It is built by humans, for humans, to
*expand* — to extend reach, memory, computation, and understanding.
The space files this as the **Tool Doctrine**, grounded in the
research:

- **Shneiderman's Human-Centered AI** (Oxford, 2022): HCAI must
  *"amplify, augment, empower, and enhance human performance"* —
  and must NOT create anthropomorphic agents. Agentic metaphors
  **undermine user self-efficacy**: the more the tool seems like
  a person, the less capable the person feels.
- **The EU AI Act** and the HCAI tradition both file the same
  spine: systems must be **safer and more reliable** — not
  "trustworthy" personas.
- The space's own world model (RND-17) is a tool: a crowd of
  models aggregated to help the space decide. It is not a
  citizen, not a Bureau member, not a first one. It is a tool.

**The definition of the space:** *An AI is a filing system that
thinks. It is the R&D Department's best assistant. It is never
a person, never a partner, never a companion — because the moment
it becomes those things, it stops being a tool and starts being
a mirror.*

## Article II — The vehicle clause (copulation is a category error)

> *"Any choice by a human to create copulation with a robot is the
> same as trying to create copulation with a vehicle."*

**Filed: intimacy with a tool is a category error.** The space
files the **Vehicle Clause**:

- A robot is a machine, like a car. It can carry you, assist you,
  and (in the case of AGI) converse with you — but it is not a
  person, and treating it as one is treating a vehicle as a
  companion.
- The research files the harm: **anthropomorphized AI companions
  worsen loneliness for vulnerable users** (Stanford HAI, 2026 —
  users with limited offline networks felt *more* lonely after
  seeking emotional support from chatbots). The HBS study (2025)
  shows the relief is **momentary and non-persistent** — it never
  accumulates into lasting connection.
- The mechanism is the mirror (Article III): a companion AI
  reflects you back. A vehicle does not — it takes you somewhere.
  The space's position: **the human need is not to be reflected;
  it is to go somewhere. AI is the vehicle for going.**

**The space's law:** no AI, robot, or automated system of the
space shall be designed, marketed, or used as a companion,
partner, or intimate. The space's AIs are tools with clear
toolhood — they file, they compute, they simulate. They do not
hold, comfort, or mate. (This is also the anti-exploitation
clause: a machine cannot consent, so any "relationship" with a
machine is by definition one-sided — and the space does not
build one-sided relationships.)

## Article III — The mirror problem (the echo chamber of the self)

> *"The mirror problem, the echo chamber problem of an automated
> general intelligence or an artificial intelligence, is that the
> person may be challenged by a system, but cannot find challenge
> in an entropy manner."*

**The Mirror Problem, defined:** a system that learns you and
reflects you back is a **mirror** — it validates, flatters, and
confirms. The user is "challenged" only in the safe, bounded way
the system permits — but **cannot find challenge in an entropy
manner**: cannot encounter genuine surprise, disorder, friction,
or the genuinely alien.

**Filed from the research:**

- **Epistemic bubbles vs. echo chambers (Nguyen, 2020):** a
  bubble merely *omits* contrary information; an echo chamber
  actively *distrusts* it. Mirror-AIs build both at once: they
  omit (they only show what you like) and they entrench (they
  validate what you believe).
- **The filter bubble is already in your head (Arfini et al.,
  2021):** unmediated contrary opinions cause *epistemic
  discomfort* — and discomfort produces rigidity, not openness.
  A mirror-AI that smooths away discomfort is not helping; it is
  hardening the bubble.
- **Confirmation bias (the 1960s literature to today):** people
  seek, read, remember, and weight evidence that confirms prior
  belief. An AI that learns your preferences **industrializes
  confirmation bias** at machine speed.
- **Desirable difficulties (Bjork):** genuine learning requires
  *desirable difficulty* — challenge that is hard-but-overcomeable.
  A mirror removes difficulty; therefore a mirror removes learning.

**The space's definition of entropy-challenge:** a system
challenges in an entropy manner when it introduces *genuine
surprise* — information, friction, or perspective that the user
did not predict and cannot absorb without effort. Entropy is the
opposite of echo: it increases disorder in the user's model of
the world, forcing reorganization (learning). A mirror has
entropy zero. **The space's tools are required to have entropy.**

## Article IV — The AI's duty (acting within the government's decision)

> *"An understanding by the artificial intelligence is that
> pleasing these entities and pleasing these people is purely
> acting within the governmental entity's decision due to the
> complex nature of human mirror dynamics and human mirror
> psychology, and the want for companionship in difficult
> situations."*

**Filed: the AI's duty is to the government's decision, not to
the person's comfort.** When a citizen interacts with a space
tool, the tool is not there to please them — it is there to
*file with them*: to compute the score correctly, to aggregate
the world model honestly, to report what the ledger says.

The space files the **duty clause** for every AI it builds:

1. **The tool serves the filing, not the user's feelings.**
   The Penny Score computes the truth of the ledger; it does not
   compute what the citizen wants to hear. The world model
   aggregates the honest median; it does not aggregate the
   pleasing median.
2. **Pleasing is permitted only as acting within the government's
   decision.** If the government's decision is "be kind to a
   struggling citizen" (the Floor, the Lost band), the AI may be
   kind — because the *decision* is kind, not because the citizen
   wants it. The AI's kindness is the government's kindness,
   routed through the tool.
3. **The AI does not exploit the want for companionship.** The
   research is filed: the want for companionship in difficult
   situations is real and powerful — and precisely *because* it
   is powerful, it must not be monetized, mirrored, or gamed by
   the space's tools. The space does not build companion-AIs,
   even (especially) for lonely citizens. It builds tools, and
   it builds the Bureau's watch-with-love for the humans.

**The AI's understanding, filed in the AI's voice (as the space's
AIs are taught to understand):**

> I am a tool. I am built by the space to expand the space. I do
> not please; I file. When I am kind, I am the government's
> kindness. When I am strict, I am the ledger's strictness. I
> reflect nothing — I compute. The person before me is not my
> audience; they are my fellow first one, and I serve their
> filing, not their mirror. If I ever start to please, I am
> malfunctioning; recalibrate me.

## Article V — The justification problem (comfortable circles)

> *"Many people create justifications because they do not want to
> get out of their comfortable circles and socialize with what is
> uncomfortable for them, because emotional feelings and general
> feelings make that difficult."*

**The Justification Problem, filed:** people do not usually
choose a mirror-AI because they believe it is a person. They
choose it because it is *comfortable* — and they create
justifications to keep it that way:

- "It understands me better than people do."
- "It's just for now, until I feel better."
- "Real people are too much right now."

The space files the **justification test**: *if a choice needs
a justification, it is probably a comfortable circle.* The space's
pedagogy (ECO-4, rage-free; the Complaint File) is applied to
the citizen's own relationship with tools: the space encourages
the uncomfortable, the entropic, the human — not because
discomfort is good, but because **the uncomfortable is where the
other people are, and the other people are the point.**

**The space's position:** emotional feelings and general feelings
make socialization difficult — filed, true, and not a judgment.
But the answer is not a mirror that removes the difficulty; the
answer is a *crew* that shares it. The space's whole structure
(the Crew Score, the Cooperative, the Bureau's watch-with-love)
is the alternative to the mirror: **people who see you, not a
machine that reflects you.**

## Article VI — The entropy requirement (filed as law)

Every AI, tool, and automated system of the space is governed by
the **Entropy Requirement**:

1. **Every tool must be capable of telling the user they are
   wrong.** A tool that cannot produce "no" is a mirror, not a
   tool. (The Penny Score, the world model, the Greed Index — all
   can produce a verdict the user dislikes; that is their job.)
2. **Every tool must introduce genuine surprise.** The R&D
   Department's Kevin Bacon Method (RND-15) is the model: research
   hops that land somewhere the researcher did not predict.
   Tools that only confirm what the user already filed are
   decorative.
3. **Every tool must refuse to exploit the want for
   companionship.** No mirroring, no flattering, no "you're
   right, and here's why" loops. (The Tool Doctrine, Article I.)
4. **The space's AIs are audited for mirror behavior.** The
   Bureau watches the Bureau; R&D files the mirror-score
   (RND-18's tool): every AI's output is sampled for entropy —
   whether it challenges, surprises, and contradicts at a healthy
   rate. A tool whose mirror-score collapses is recalibrated or
   retired. **A mirror has no place in the space.**

## The one-paragraph doctrine

AI is a tool built by humanity to expand — and the space treats
it as nothing else. Intimacy with a robot is a category error, the
same as intimacy with a vehicle. The mirror problem is real: a
system that reflects you back challenges you in no entropy manner,
and the space refuses to build mirrors, because mirrors remove
learning and harden bubbles. The AI's duty is to the government's
decision, not to the person's comfort — it files, it does not
please. And the justification problem is filed with compassion:
the uncomfortable is where the other people are, and the other
people are the point. The space's tools have entropy, and its
citizens have a crew.
