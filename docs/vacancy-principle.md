# The Vacancy Principle — Behavioral Economics of Pricing and Humility

**Government filing GOV-71. Research verified: Vacancy costs exceed
rent reduction costs (Croskey Real Estate, Facebook property
management data). Loss aversion (Kahneman & Tversky) explains why
landlords reject lower offers. DSCR constraints (Investopedia,
JPMorgan) create structural pressure. The ~ WuBu ~ space adopts
this principle for token economics and base economy design.**

---

## Part 1 — The Insight

### 1.1 — The Tweet

Jon Brooks (@jonbrooks, Sep 14, 2026):

> "A landlord rejects $2,300/month because he 'needs' $2,500.
> The property sits vacant for 6 weeks.
> He just lost more money than the higher rent would've earned
> him all year.
> Vacancy is more expensive than humility."

### 1.2 — The Math

| Scenario | Monthly Rent | Annual Income | Vacancy Cost |
|----------|--------------|---------------|--------------|
| **Accept $2,300** | $2,300 | $27,600 | $0 |
| **Hold for $2,500** | $2,500 | $26,250 | -$3,465 (6 weeks) |
| **Net Difference** | +$200/mo | -$1,350/yr | -$3,465 |

**The landlord lost $3,465 to gain $2,400/year. Net loss: $1,065.**

*Note: 6 weeks vacancy at $2,500/mo = $3,465 lost rent. The "Hold" scenario collects rent for only 10.5 months (46 weeks), not 11 months.*

### 1.3 — The Behavioral Error

The landlord suffers from **loss aversion** (Kahneman & Tversky,
Prospect Theory):
- The pain of accepting $200 less feels larger than the gain of
  $2,700/year in reduced vacancy.
- He anchors to $2,500 as the "fair" price.
- He overestimates the probability of finding a $2,500 tenant.
- He underestimates the cost of 6 weeks of vacancy.

## Part 2 — The Research

### 2.1 — Vacancy Cost Data

Research verified (Croskey Real Estate, Facebook property
management, SoloLandlordTools):

| Vacancy Duration | Cost at $2,300/mo | Cost at $2,750/mo |
|------------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| 1 week | $575 | $633 |
| 3 weeks | $1,725 | $1,899 |
| 6 weeks | $3,450 | $3,797 |
| 12 weeks | $6,900 | $7,594 |

**Rule of thumb: 6 weeks vacancy = 1.5 months of rent lost.**

### 2.2 — DSCR Constraints

Research verified (Investopedia, JPMorgan, Truss Financial,
LendMire):

- **DSCR = Gross Rental Income ÷ Annual Debt Service**
- **Minimum DSCR: 1.20-1.25** (most lenders)
- **DSCR < 1.0 = negative cash flow**

The reply from @ziggy33lee:
> "It could be because a lot of rental properties are set up on
> DSCR loans which have to meet certain debt to income per
> property. So if a property cost $2,000 a month to hold after
> insurance, reserve, maintenance, management, the bank wants
> to see $500 of cash flow to hold the loan."

**This is a structural constraint, not just behavioral.**

### 2.3 — The Reply Nuance

@Ryan_Husmann:
> "More to it than that. It also depends on the quality of the
> tenant. If I lower the rent and get a bad tenant in the place
> that destroys it… it could end up costing more."

**This is valid: adverse selection in rental markets.**

## Part 3 — The ~ WuBu ~ Application

### 3.1 — The Token Economy Parallel

The vacancy principle applies directly to the space's token
economics:

| Landlord Mistake | Space Equivalent |
|------------------|------------------|
| Reject $2,300 for $2,500 | Reject a lower-value task for a higher-value one |
| 6 weeks vacancy | 6 weeks of no cooperation |
| Loss aversion | Status quo bias in mission selection |
| DSCR constraints | Minimum cooperation score requirements |

### 3.2 — The Base Economy Lesson

The base economy (GOV-33) must account for vacancy costs:

1. **Tasks must be priced to fill, not to maximize.** A task
   priced at 10 WT that sits uncompleted for 6 weeks costs
   more than a task priced at 5 WT that is completed
   immediately.

2. **Citizens must be activated, not optimized.** A citizen
   who completes 5 low-value tasks contributes more than a
   citizen who waits for 1 high-value task.

3. **The worm game must reward completion, not aspiration.**
   The worm grows when fed, not when waiting for better food.

### 3.3 — The Humility Principle

The space adopts the **Humility Principle**:

> "Vacancy is more expensive than humility."

In practice:
- **Accept lower-value cooperation** rather than wait for
  higher-value cooperation.
- **Price tasks for completion**, not for maximization.
- **Reward showing up**, not waiting for perfection.
- **The meek inherit** because they act; the proud wait.

### 3.4 — The Anti-Vacancy Rule

The space's anti-vacancy rule:

1. **Every task has a floor price** (minimum WT to complete).
2. **Every task has a ceiling price** (maximum WT to complete).
3. **The actual price is set to ensure completion**, not to
   maximize value.
4. **If a task sits uncompleted for 30 days, the price drops
   by 10%.**
5. **If a task sits uncompleted for 60 days, the price drops
   by 25%.**
6. **If a task sits uncompleted for 90 days, it is removed
   from the Mission Board.**

## Part 4 — The Behavioral Economics Integration

### 4.1 — Loss Aversion in the Space

Citizens suffer from loss aversion:
- They overvalue their existing tokens.
- They undervalue future cooperation.
- They anchor to past mission rewards.
- They fear losing status more than they value gaining it.

The space's answer:
- **Make cooperation visible** (the worm grows).
- **Make defection visible** (the worm shrinks).
- **Make the cost of waiting explicit** (the mission board
  shows "days uncompleted").
- **Make the benefit of action explicit** (the mission board
  shows "WT earned").

### 4.2 — Prospect Theory in the Space

Prospect Theory (Kahneman & Tversky):
- People feel losses 2x more than equivalent gains.
- People are risk-averse in gains, risk-seeking in losses.

The space's answer:
- **Frame missions as gains** ("Earn 10 WT") not as losses
  ("Lose 10 WT if you don't complete").
- **Frame inaction as loss** ("This task expires in 7 days").
- **Frame cooperation as the default** ("Your worm is hungry").

### 4.3 — The DSCR Parallel

The space's DSCR equivalent:
- **Cooperation Score = Verified Actions ÷ Expected Actions**
- **Minimum Cooperation Score: 0.5** (below this, powers unlock)
- **Target Cooperation Score: 1.0** (full powers)
- **DSCR < 0.5 = "negative cash flow"** (citizen is net
  consumer, not net contributor)

## Part 5 — Operations

### 5.1 — The Vacancy Auditor

The **Vacancy Auditor** monitors the Mission Board:
- Tracks days uncompleted per task.
- Flags tasks approaching the 30-day threshold.
- Recommends price adjustments.
- Reports to the founder monthly.

### 5.2 — The Pricing Committee

The **Pricing Committee** sets task prices:
- Uses the vacancy principle to set floor/ceiling.
- Adjusts prices based on completion data.
- Ensures the base economy is "full employment" for tasks.

### 5.3 — The Humility Officer

The **Humility Officer** ensures the space follows the Humility
Principle:
- Monitors for "prideful" pricing (tasks priced too high).
- Monitors for "aspirational" missions (missions that sound
  good but never get completed).
- Reports to the founder quarterly.
