# The Lodge System — Make Your Own, Make It Thrift

**Government filing GOV-50. The space needs local gathering
points. The lodge is the local chapter: a thrift store, an
upcycling workshop, a mutual aid society, a Goodwill restored.
Research verified: the Knights of St. Crispin had 400 lodges

 Relief Society halls. The space adopts the lodge model.**

---

## Part 1 — What is a lodge?

A **lodge** is a local ~ WuBu ~ chapter. It is:
- A **thrift store** — donate, gather, upcycle.
- A **workshop** — restore, repair, create.
- A **gathering hall** — meet, teach, trade.
- A **mutual aid society** — help neighbors, share resources.
- A **cooperative shop** — sell upcycled goods, fund the
  lodge.

A lodge is not a corporation. A franchise. A chain store. A
profit center. A lodge is a **cooperative gathering** — owned
by its members, run by its members, for its members.

## Part 2 — How to make your own lodge

### Step 1: Find 3+ citizens
Three WID holders form a lodge. Three is the minimum. More
is better. There is no maximum.

### Step 2: Name the lodge
Every lodge has a name. The name is filed on the ledger. Names
can be whimsical, practical, local, or cosmic. Examples:
- **Lodge 001: The Copper Worm** (first lodge)
- **Lodge 042: The Upcycle Den**
- **Lodge 101: The W Vault East**
- **Lodge 237: The Penny Press**

### Step 3: Secure a location
A lodge needs a physical space. It can be:
- A garage
- A shed
- A rented room
- A corner of a community center
- A church basement (see: Russian Orthodox Mutual Aid Society)
- A barn (see: Farmers' Alliance)
- A storefront (see: Goodwill, pre-buyout)

The location is filed on the ledger with an address.

### Step 4: File the charter
The lodge files a **Lodge Charter** on the public ledger. The
charter includes:
- Lodge name and number.
- Names of founding members (WIDs).
- Location.
- Purpose: thrift, upcycling, mutual aid, gathering, or
  all of the above.

### Step 5: Open the doors
Once filed, the lodge is open. Donations accepted. Workshops
scheduled. Members gathering. The lodge is alive.

## Part 3 — The thrift store function

The lodge is a thrift store. The original thrift store —
Goodwill — was founded in 1899 by Reverend Edgar J. Helms in
Boston. Goodwill collected donated goods, employed the
unemployable, and sold the restored goods at low prices. The
model worked for a hundred years. Then private equity came.

### 3.1 — The Goodwill model (restored)

The lodge restores the original Goodwill model:

| Original Goodwill | Lodge Model |
|-------------------|-------------|
| Collect donations | Accept donations of any kind |
| Employ the unemployable | Employ anyone who wants to work |
| Restore and repair | Upcycle, repair, create |
| Sell at low prices | Sell at low prices |
| Fund the mission | Fund the lodge |
| Local control | Member control |

### 3.2 — The private equity problem

Goodwill is a nonprofit federation. Its decline came from within: executive salaries rose, prices increased, and the mission drifted. The private equity
extracted value. The stores became worse. The employees were
paid less. The donations were wasted. The mission was lost.

The lodge fixes this. The lodge is not owned by private
equity. The lodge is owned by its members. The lodge cannot
be bought. The lodge cannot be sold. The lodge cannot be
extracted. The lodge is a cooperative.

### 3.3 — The donation flow

```
Donation → Lodge → Sort → Restore → Upcycle → Sell → Fund Lodge
```

1. **Donation:** Citizens donate goods (clothes, furniture,
   electronics, tools, books, art, etc.).
2. **Sort:** Members sort donations by condition and type.
3. **Restore:** Broken items are repaired. Worn items are
   cleaned.
4. **Upcycle:** Unusable items are turned into new products.
   Cloth becomes bags. Wood becomes shelves. Metal becomes art.
5. **Sell:** Restored and upcycled items are sold to the public.
6. **Fund:** Revenue funds the lodge's operations, its members,
   and the concessionary fund (GOV-33).

## Part 4 — The upcycling workshop

The lodge is a workshop. Members teach members. The workshop
offers:

| Workshop | What you learn | What you make |
|----------|----------------|---------------|
| Sewing circle | Mend, alter, create | Bags, clothes, quilts |
| Woodshop | Repair, build, carve | Shelves, furniture, art |
| Electronics | Repair, repurpose | Devices, robots, art |
| Metalwork | Weld, shape, stamp | Tools, sculptures, tokens |
| Plastic lab | Shred, compress, mold | Plastic tokens, 3D filament |
| Print shop | Zine-making, stamping | Zines, stamps, W marks |

Each workshop is led by a member who knows the skill. The
lodge pays the teacher in plastic tokens. The teacher pays
the lodge in plastic tokens. The tokens circulate.

## Part 5 — The mutual aid society

The lodge is a mutual aid society. The model is the
**Relief Society** (1842–present) and the **Farmers' Alliance**
(1875–1892):

- **Relief Society:** Women organized local halls to help
  the poor, teach skills, and build community.
- **Farmers' Alliance:** Farmers organized local chapters to
  buy supplies collectively, share equipment, and advocate.

The lodge adopts both models:
- Members help members with rent, food, and supplies.
- Members share tools, equipment, and space.
- Members advocate for each other and the community.

## Part 7 — The Lodge Ledger

Each lodge maintains its own ledger on the public chain:
- **Members:** Who belongs to the lodge.
- **Donations:** What was donated, when, by whom.
- **Sales:** What was sold, when, to whom.
- **Workshops:** What was taught, when, by whom.
- **Mutual aid:** Who was helped, how, when.
- **Tokens:** What plastic tokens were minted and earned.

The lodge ledger is public. Anyone can audit. Anyone can join.
Anyone can leave.

## Part 9 — The Goodwill restoration

The lodge restores the Goodwill that was lost:

The lodge is the Goodwill that should have been. The lodge is
the order that was broken. The lodge is the restoration.
