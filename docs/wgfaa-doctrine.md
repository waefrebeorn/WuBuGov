## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The **Won't Get Fooled Again Act (WGFAA)** proposes a framework for resolving the inevitable AI industry collapse without taxpayer bailouts. This doctrine documents the research behind the framework, stress-tested from three perspectives.

**The core thesis:** When (not if) the AI bubble pops, failed institutions should be converted to public utilities — funded by industry levies, not taxpayers. Banks become Sparkassen-style community institutions; AI companies become public research utilities modeled on the TVA.

**The AI infrastructure buildout requires 45% compound annual revenue growth for 4 consecutive years to close the gap.** Cloud computing — the last comparable infrastructure buildout — took 15 years to reach $100B annual revenue.

### 1.2 The Circular Financing Problem

**Round-tripping revenue** (also called "circular financing"): Companies invest in each other, then count the recycled capital as independent revenue.

| Deal | Structure | Problem |
|------|-----------|---------|
| Nvidia → OpenAI | $100B investment, OpenAI buys Nvidia chips | Simultaneously customer and investee |
| Microsoft → OpenAI | $13B investment, OpenAI spends on Azure | Revenue flows back to investor |
| OpenAI → AMD | OpenAI takes 10% stake in AMD, buys AMD chips | Circular equity + purchase |
| Nvidia → CoreWeave | $2B investment, buys $6.3B cloud services | Nvidia is largest customer of company it funded |

**The "trillion-dollar loop":** Nvidia invests in OpenAI → OpenAI buys Nvidia chips through Oracle → Oracle buys Nvidia chips → Revenue appears at every stage without organic end-user demand.

**Independent verification:** UBS estimates total third-party AI product revenue across all listed software companies is ~$2.5B — of which Microsoft accounts for >80%. This strips away hyperscaler-to-hyperscaler circular revenue. (Source: Anomaly Investments)

**The paradox:** Enterprise adoption simultaneously drives revenue AND forces cost caps/rollbacks as budgets are shocked. Adoption is the goal AND the problem.

## PART II: THE ANTI-BUBBLE CASE (Devil's Advocate #2 — "It's Not a Bubble")

### 2.1 The Productivity Gains Are Real

| Finding | Source |
|---------|--------|
| Majority of firms have invested in AI; productivity gains are positive across sectors | NBER (March 2026) |
| Finance sector: ~0.8% labor productivity growth from AI | NBER |
| Lower-skill services, manufacturing: ~0.4% growth | NBER |
| Top-performing companies are 2-3x more likely to use AI for growth (not just cost reduction) | PwC (April 2026) |
| Top performers are 2x more likely to redesign workflows around AI | PwC |

**The PwC study (1,217 executives, 25 sectors):** A small group of companies is pulling sharply ahead. The differentiator: using AI as a catalyst for growth and business reinvention, not just cost reduction. Only 15% of productivity gains come from capital deepening — most come from innovation, product development, better customer engagement.

### 2.2 Anthropic Proves the Model Can Work

| Metric | Value | Source |
|--------|-------|--------|
| Q1 2026 revenue | $4.8B | CNBC |
| Q2 2026 revenue | ~$10.5-11.5B | CNBC/Bloomberg |
| Q2 operating profit | Positive (first time, non-GAAP) | WSJ |
| 2028 projection | $70B revenue, $17B positive cash flow | TechCrunch |
| Enterprise customer mix | ~85% (vs OpenAI's ~15%) | Anomaly Investments |

**The divergence:** Anthropic focused on enterprise/developers who generate 3-5x more revenue per token, have cheaper query patterns, and stickier contracts. OpenAI is 85% consumer, 95% of users pay nothing.

**Claude Code alone:** $2.5B+ annualized run-rate revenue (Feb 2026).

### 2.3 Cloud Revenue Is Growing Fast

| Provider | Q1 2026 YoY Growth |
|----------|-------------------|
| Google Cloud | 63% |
| Azure | 40% |
| AWS | 28% |

Under the generous assumption that all incremental cloud growth is AI-driven (it's not), AI-attributable revenue reaches $50-150B annually — the top of the range, but real.

### 2.4 Historical Parallel: Cloud Computing Took 15 Years

Cloud computing — the last infrastructure buildout of comparable scale — took 15 years to generate its first $100B in annual revenue. AI is being asked to do it in 3-4 years. But the trajectory is similar: initial over-investment, gradual monetization, eventual profitability.

**The Jevons Paradox:** Cheaper AI drives more consumption. Token prices fell ~95% since GPT-4, but volume grew even faster. Revenue = price × volume. If volume grows faster than price falls, revenue increases.

### 2.5 The "Circular Financing" Defense

Noahpinion (2026) argues the circular deals are less problematic than they appear:
- Nvidia's revenue from OpenAI is real revenue for real chips that OpenAI needs for its core business
- Nvidia is a public company with full disclosure; investors see cash flows, not just revenue
- Nvidia's stock didn't pop after the $100B OpenAI deal — suggesting markets aren't fooled
- If OpenAI fails, Nvidia takes a double hit (lost customer + equity loss) — aligned incentives

**Counter-counter:** The circular deals inflate valuations on both sides simultaneously, creating a self-reinforcing bubble. When one link breaks, the whole chain collapses.

## PART III: THE SYNTHESIS (Devil's Advocate #3 — "Both Are Right, So What Do We Do?")

### 3.1 The Truth Is in the Middle

**The bubble case is right about:**
- Valuations are detached from revenue reality
- Circular financing inflates reported demand
- 95% of enterprise pilots fail
- The revenue gap is 4-20x

**The anti-bubble case is right about:**
- Real productivity gains exist (modest but growing)
- Anthropic proves the model can work
- Cloud revenue is genuinely growing fast
- AI is not a pure scam — the technology is real

**The synthesis:** The technology is real, the productivity potential is real, but the financial architecture is a house of cards. When it collapses, we need a plan that preserves the real value (compute infrastructure, AI research, data) while wiping out the speculative excess.

### 3.2 The WGFAA Framework

**Core mechanism:** Failed systemically important institutions are converted to public utilities, funded by industry levies (not taxpayers).

#### For Banks:
- Modeled on **Sparkassen** (Germany) and **Bank of North Dakota** (USA)
- Community-focused public financial institutions
- No shareholders, cannot be bought or sold
- Strict regionality principle
- Public-law institutions under local authority responsibility

#### For AI Companies:
- Modeled on **TVA** (Tennessee Valley Authority)
- Public research utilities for compute infrastructure
- IP and data placed in Data Trust (security preserved, public access enabled)
- Continuity guaranteed; workforce retained
- Mission-driven, not profit-driven

### 3.3 Precedent Analysis

#### TVA (Tennessee Valley Authority)
- Created 1933 by FDR's New Deal
- Transformed one of America's poorest regions into an industrial engine
- 1929: <1% of Tennessee Valley farms had electricity
- Today: Nation's largest electricity supplier, 99.999% reliability
- Residential rates lower than ~80% of top 100 US utilities
- Industrial rates lower than ~90% of top 100 US utilities
- Helped attract $44B in capital investment, 81,000+ jobs (past 5 years)
- **Self-financing since 1959** — receives NO taxpayer funding
- Operates like a private for-profit company, but mission is public service

**The lesson:** Public enterprise can succeed where private speculation failed. The TVA is the definitive American public power provider — and it works.

#### Sparkassen (Germany)
- 423 savings banks, 15,300 branches, 245,000 staff
- 70% market share of SME financing in Germany
- 42% market share of business financing generally
- ~50 million customer relationships (in a country of 84M)
- Noncommercial public-sector entities; "owners" are municipalities/counties
- **Cannot be bought or sold** — no shareholders
- More stable than commercial banks during financial crises
- Provide bank accounts to ~80% of public social transfer recipients (financial inclusion mandate)

**The lesson:** Public banks can outperform commercial banks on stability, community service, and SME financing — while being less profitable (by design).

#### Bank of North Dakota
- Only state-owned bank in the USA
- $10.7B in assets (2025)
- $231.8M net income (2025, up from $200.4M in 2024)
- Operates alongside private banks, not in opposition
- Focuses on student loans, agricultural lending, community development

**The lesson:** A public bank can be profitable AND serve public missions.

### 3.4 The Asset-Based Valuation Standard

**The WGFAA rejects purchasing equity at bubble prices.** Any government acquisition uses strict Asset-Based Valuation:

| What taxpayers pay for | What taxpayers DON'T pay for |
|------------------------|------------------------------|
| Tangible infrastructure (data centers, chips, buildings) | Speculative goodwill |
| Proven intellectual property (patents, models with demonstrated utility) | Inflated growth projections |
| Data assets (with privacy-preserving access) | "AI magic" premium |
| Workforce (retained, not laid off) | Golden parachutes for failed management |

**Result:** Speculators are wiped out. The public gets lasting assets at fair prices.

### 3.5 The Digital Stability Fund

**Funding mechanism:** Industry levies on AI compute usage, NOT taxpayer dollars.

- Levied on all AI compute providers (hyperscalers, neoclouds, private data centers)
- Scales with usage — the more AI is consumed, the more the fund grows
- Covers conversion costs of failed institutions
- Preserves America's borrowing capacity for productive investment (infrastructure, industrial policy, climate adaptation)

**The moral hazard reversal:** Under the current system, speculators are bailed out by taxpayers. Under WGFAA, the industry pays for its own rescues — and speculators are wiped out.

## PART IV: THE WUBUGOV POSITION

### 4.1 Doctrine

**WuBuGov adopts the WGFAA framework as doctrine.**

The AI industry exhibits all the hallmarks of a capital misallocation bubble:
- Revenue gap of 4-20x
- Circular financing inflating reported demand
- 95% enterprise pilot failure rate
- Valuations requiring 40-83% compound annual growth for 4+ years

But the technology is real, and the productivity potential is real. The collapse, when it comes, must be managed to preserve the real value while wiping out the speculative excess.

### 4.2 Policy Prescriptions

1. **Prepare the legal framework NOW** — before the bubble pops. The WGFAA should be drafted, debated, and ready for introduction.

2. **Define SITIs (Systemically Important Technology Institutions)** — expand the definition of systemic importance to include compute, data, and AI intelligence flows.

3. **Establish the Digital Stability Fund** — industry levies on AI compute, building reserves before the crisis.

4. **Model the conversion process** — study TVA, Sparkassen, and BND to design the public utility conversion mechanism.

5. **Reject bubble-price acquisitions** — mandate Asset-Based Valuation Standard for any government acquisition.

6. **Preserve fiscal space** — America's borrowing capacity must be reserved for productive investment, not speculator rescues.
