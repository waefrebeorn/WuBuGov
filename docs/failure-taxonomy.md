# The Failure Taxonomy — Lessons from Collapsed Systems

**Government filing GOV-73. Research verified: Platform cooperatives
fail from cost traps, governance attacks, volunteer burnout, and
exit-to-fiat leaks. This filing documents the failure modes so the
~ WuBuGov ~ space can avoid them.**

---

## Part 1 — Platform Cooperative Failures

### 1.1 — The Cost Trap

**Ampled** (artist music platform): Burnout from underpaid labor,
inferior product, inability to compete. Co-founder: "The products
in the platform co-op space aren't good."

**The Drivers Cooperative** (NYC rideshare): $M spent on app
development caused "blown head gasket" between engineers and
drivers; lost access to own platform.

**Lesson for WuBuGov:** Budget for sustainable tech, not maximal
tech. The tools should be good enough, not perfect.

### 1.2 — The Legitimacy Trap

**Co-op Atlantic** (100-year-old Canadian co-op): Centralized
decision-making destroyed legitimacy; copied Walmart instead of
leveraging co-op identity.

**Lesson for WuBuGov:** The lodge system (GOV-50) must be genuinely
local. If lodges feel like branches of a central organization,
legitimacy collapses.

### 1.3 — The Succession Trap

**SMart Belgium + Deliveroo**: Platform abandoned cooperative
when convenient; collective representation too weak.

**Lewes Pound** (17-year community currency): Closed due to no
succession — "unable to find people to take it over."

**Lesson for WuBuGov:** GOV-64 succession is critical. Without it,
the space dies with the founder.

## Part 2 — DAO Governance Attacks ($200M+ in documented losses)

### 2.1 — The Flash Loan Coup

**Beanstalk (2022):** $182M via flash loan + zero-timelock
emergency governance (13-second coup). The attacker borrowed
voting power, passed a malicious proposal, and drained the
treasury before anyone could react.

**Lesson for WuBuGov:** If governance is token-weighted, a wealthy
attacker can buy voting power. The cooperation-score weighting
(GOV-17) is a partial defense, but not complete.

### 2.2 — The Hostile Takeover

**Build Finance (2022):** Hostile takeover via token accumulation;
attacker "did not break any rules."

**Steemit (2020):** Justin Sun bought 30% of tokens + exchange
collusion to capture the network.

**Lesson for WuBuGov:** Token-weighted governance is vulnerable to
wealth concentration. The anti-meta-gaming doctrine (GOV-63) must
be enforced.

### 2.3 — The Voter Apathy Attack

**Solend/Juno/Arbitrum:** Voter apathy (0.33% turnout), Sybil
attacks, governance clog.

**Lesson for WuBuGov:** Low turnout means a small group can control
votes. The quorum requirement (GOV-17) is a partial defense.

## Part 3 — Community Currency Collapses

### 3.1 — The Circulation Failure

**Bristol Pound:** 9 years, modest circulation, administrative
costs exceeded impact.

**Chiemgauer, Japanese currencies:** Circulation failure, lack of
acceptance, administrative burnout.

**Lesson for WuBuGov:** The Penny Clause (GOV-47) must generate
enough volume to sustain the base economy. If pennies are too
rare, the base economy starves.

### 3.2 — The Exit-to-Fiat Leak

**Circles UBI:** Launched with 100K+ users but FAILED: businesses
cashed out 90% to EUR, price gouging emerged, scalability
issues — closed Jan 2024.

**Lesson for WuBuGov:** If plastic tokens can be sold for fiat,
the circular economy leaks. The anti-meta-gaming doctrine (GOV-63)
must prevent token-to-fiat exchanges.

## Part 4 — Mitigation Strategies

### 4.1 — For Platform Cost

1. **Start with markdown, not apps.** The filings ARE the
   government. Tools are secondary.
2. **Budget for maintenance, not growth.** A small, working system
   beats a large, broken one.
3. **Use other people's servers.** The space uses GitHub, X,
   YouTube, DuckDuckGo — not its own infrastructure.

### 4.2 — For Governance Attacks

1. **Cooperation-score weighting.** Votes are weighted by
   cooperation, not just tokens.
2. **Quorum requirements.** Minimum participation before votes
   bind.
3. **Time-locked proposals.** No instant governance changes.
4. **Founder veto (temporary).** Until the space matures, the
   founder can veto malicious proposals.

### 4.3 — For Currency Collapse

1. **Closed-loop incentives.** Tokens must be spendable within
   the system, not just exchangeable for fiat.
2. **Lodge-level circulation.** Lodges create local demand for
   tokens.
3. **Task-based issuance.** Tokens are earned through tasks, not
   bought (except Fast Track).

### 4.4 — For Succession

1. **GOV-64 succession plan.** Already filed.
2. **Multi-sig transition.** As the space matures, founder powers
   transition to multi-sig.
3. **Lodge autonomy.** Lodges can continue even if the central
   government fails.
