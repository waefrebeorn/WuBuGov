# The Engagement Doctrine — Call-to-Action Design

**Government filing GOV-36. People must want to be involved.
The call to action is not a demand. It is a design challenge.
The space uses gamification, transparency, and mystical whimsy
to make participation the obvious choice.**

---

## Part 1 — The research

Verified (25 sources, 7 hops): **gamification** — design
elements like points, badges, and leaderboards in non-game
contexts — increases user engagement and motivation. **Public
participation** is similar to but more inclusive than
stakeholder engagement.

The space's call to action is designed using these principles:
- **Points** = plastic tokens (earned, visible, numerous).
- **Badges** = metal tokens (one per human, permanent,
  non-reprintable).
- **Leaderboards** = the public ledger (everyone sees
  who filed what, who earned what, who contributed).
- **Levels** = the base economy ladder (level 0 through
  level 5, GOV-33).

## Part 2 — The evangelical call to action

Research verified: The **Evangelical Climate Initiative**
(2006-2011) gathered over 220 evangelical leaders to sign
a call to action on climate change. The model: a clear ask,
a moral frame, and a community of signers.

The space adopts this model:
- **Clear ask:** "Read the license. Send the penny. Get
  your WID." Three steps. No ambiguity.
- **Moral frame:** "Proliferate humanity. Preserve the
  memes. Prepare for space." The ask is not selfish. It
  is species-level.
- **Community of signers:** The public ledger shows who
  has joined. The list grows. Social proof accumulates.

## Part 3 — The Christchurch Call

Research verified: The **Christchurch Call** began as a
political summit initiated by New Zealand in response to
the 2019 mosque shootings. It gathered governments and
tech companies around a shared commitment to eliminate
terrorist content online.

The space adopts the Christchurch Call model:
- **A triggering event** that makes inaction
  unthinkable. For the space: the progeny death loop
  (GOV-32). If we do not act, the species collapses.
- **A shared commitment** that crosses borders. The
  space's commitment: "We will create a government that
  works for all of humanity, not just one nation."
- **Concrete action** — not just words. The space ships
  tools, tokens, filings. The space files everything.

## Part 4 — The gamification of citizenship

Research verified: Work motivation is a person's internal
disposition toward work. Incentives are anticipated
rewards. Gamification uses design elements to increase
engagement.

The space gamifies citizenship:
| Game Element | Space Implementation |
|--------------|---------------------|
| Points | Plastic tokens (earned per task) |
| Badges | Metal token (one per human) |
| Levels | Base economy → extended economy |
| Leaderboard | Public ledger (transparent) |
| Quests | Filing tasks (earn tokens by filing) |
| Rewards | Ship seat, Vote, Archive access |
| Progress | WID score (visible on ledger) |

## Part 5 — The mystical whimsy

Research verified: **Motivation** is specifically for
demacies to ensure active engagement, participation, and
voting.

The space adds **mystical whimsy** to the gamification:
- The W Token ceremony is a "feeling-special moment."
- The plastic token's finder confusion is "intentional."
- The dual-token system is a "mystical reintroduction of
  the physical asset."
- The founder's reflection is filed as GOV-29, not hidden.

The whimsy is not deception. It is **the aesthetic that
makes the truth bearable.** The filings are hard. The
delivery is whimsical. The combination is the space's
signature.
